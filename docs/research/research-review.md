# Literature & Research Review: Heat Exposure, Thermal Stress & Mortality in India

This review summarizes peer-reviewed epidemiological and biometeorological research linking heat exposure, thermal indices (UTCI, WBGT, HI), and health outcomes across Indian cities.

| Paper / Reference | Location & Scope | Dataset / Timeframe | Metric Evaluated | Key Empirical Finding | Relevance to SIH26083 | Critical Limitation / Boundary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Azhar et al. (2014)** *PLoS ONE* [PMID: 24632867] | Ahmedabad, Gujarat | 2009–2011 daily all-cause mortality (AMC) | Max Dry-Bulb Temp ($T_{max}$), Heat Index | 43.1% excess mortality observed during the May 2010 heatwave (1,344 excess deaths in 7 days; peak $T_{max} = 46.8^\circ\text{C}$). | Demonstrates that extreme heat creates catastrophic mortality surges in urban Indian populations; foundational basis for Ahmedabad Heat Action Plan. | Relies on city-wide scalar temperature; did not have ward-level physiological radiative stress or real-time predictive lead times. |
| **Mazdiyasni et al. (2017)** *PNAS* [PMID: 28584104] | Pan-India (395 stations) | 1960–2009 IMD gridded temperature & EM-DAT records | Mean summer temperature, Heatwave duration | A $0.5^\circ\text{C}$ increase in mean summer temperature led to a 146% increase in the probability of heat-related mortality events exceeding 100 deaths. | Proves extreme non-linear vulnerability of Indian populations to compounding heat events and multi-day duration persistence. | Country-wide macro scale; lacks hyper-local sub-city vulnerability attribution. |
| **Mora et al. (2017)** *Nature Climate Change* [DOI: 10.1038/nclimate3322] | Global (including South Asia) | 1980–2014 documented lethal heat events | Daily mean surface temperature and relative humidity | Identified global threshold curve where combination of temperature and humidity becomes lethal due to metabolic heat accumulation. | Provides physiological theoretical basis for combining moisture ($RH$) with temperature to define survivability envelopes. | Broad global grid ($0.5^\circ$); does not account for local urban microclimate or socio-economic coping capacity. |
| **Sherwood & Huber (2010)** *PNAS* [PMID: 20439769] | Global Climatology | Global thermodynamic limit | Wet-bulb temperature ($T_w = 35^\circ\text{C}$) | Established $T_w = 35^\circ\text{C}$ as the thermodynamic threshold of human metabolic survivability for continuous exposure (>6 hours). | Scientific justification for wet-bulb / WBGT as non-negotiable human survivability threshold. | Extreme upper bound; clinical heatstroke and mortality occur at significantly lower operational thresholds ($T_w \approx 28^\circ\text{C}$ to $31^\circ\text{C}$). |
| **Błażejczyk et al. (2013)** *Geogr. Pol.* | Global Biometeorology | UTCI Commission multi-node Fiala model | Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI) | Operationalized human thermoregulation combining air temp, mean radiant temp ($T_{mrt}$), wind speed, and vapor pressure into an equivalent temperature (°C). | Primary scientific standard implemented in SIH26083 thermal engine. | Requires accurate Mean Radiant Temperature estimation, which depends on solar radiation (GHI). |
| **NCDC / MoHFW (2024)** *NPCCHH Annual Report* | National Health Surveillance | Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme (IDSP) | Heatstroke cases & suspected deaths | Formally documented over 40,000 heat-related illness cases and hundreds of deaths across India during the 2024 pre-monsoon heatwave. | Validates the urgent operational need for MoES/NCMRWF early-warning risk translation. | National aggregate surveillance lacks real-time machine-readable ward-level API feeds. |
